Lesson 01 · Quick reference

Eight ways to describe motion

For now, focus on what each word means in everyday language. Equations and calculations come later.

First, choose a reference point. For a car on a straight road, we can mark an origin and choose right as positive and left as negative. The winding road above is a real-world example; most of our first calculations will focus on straight-line motion.
Physical quantityA measurable property described by a value and an appropriate unit (and, for a vector, a direction). We often use a letter symbol for it.
SI unitsLength: metre (m). Time: second (s). Speed and velocity: m/s. Acceleration: m/s². These are SI units; a symbol is not the same as a unit.
Instant or interval?An instantaneous value describes one moment. An average value summarises motion over a period of time.
Explore by quantity type

Position

Vector

Where the car is relative to a chosen origin, including which direction it lies.

Example: The car is 20 m to the right of the origin (x = +20 m).

Distance

Scalar

The total length of the path the car has travelled, even if it changes direction.

Example: The odometer counts the ground covered.

Displacement

Vector

The change in position from where the car started to where it finished, including direction.

Example: Return to the start → zero displacement.

Speed

Scalar

How fast the car is moving at a particular moment, without specifying direction.

Example: The speedometer reads 50 km/h.

Velocity

Vector

How fast the car is moving and in which direction, at a particular moment.

Example: 50 km/h east is a velocity.

Average speed

Scalar

How fast the car travels overall during a journey, allowing for faster and slower parts.

Example: Total distance and the full journey time, including stops, both matter.

Average velocity

Vector

The overall rate of change of position, including direction, across the journey.

Example: A round trip has zero average velocity.

Acceleration

Vector

How quickly the car's velocity changes: by speeding up, slowing down, or turning.

Example: A car turning at steady speed is accelerating.
Think like a physicist

Three questions to test your understanding

Make a prediction before opening the explanation. You do not need any equations.

A car makes a complete loop and finishes where it started. Is its distance zero? Show thinking

No. The car has travelled along a path, so its distance is greater than zero. Its displacement is zero, because it finishes at its starting position.

A car travels around a bend without changing its speed. Is its velocity constant? Show thinking

No. The direction of motion changes as it turns, so the car’s velocity changes. It is accelerating, even when its speed is steady.

If a car is moving right but slowing down, which way is its acceleration? Show thinking

Its acceleration points to the left, opposite to its velocity. Acceleration tells us how velocity changes; it does not always point in the direction of travel.
