Paste from Excel: Copy one column of numbers in Excel, click the first destination cell below (such as X average or Y average), and press Ctrl+V (Windows) or ⌘V (Mac). Repeat for other columns. Rows are added automatically. You can also paste a complete X–Y or six-column range into the first X average cell.

Use 2 columns (X average, Y average), or 6 columns (all six fields), separated by tabs, commas or semicolons. Existing rows will be replaced.

Only average X and average Y are required; min/max values are optional. For a new investigation, use Clear data before pasting. Click a plotted point to mark/unmark it as an outlier, or use the table checkbox. Outliers remain visible but are excluded from calculated fits.

Leave a symbol blank to use the part of the quantity name after the final comma (e.g., Applied force, F → F). Without a comma, the default is x or y. For scientific symbols, type v_0, t^2, m s^{-2}, \Delta E_k. Subscripts, superscripts and common Greek letters are rendered on the graph.

03Best-fit line or curve

Drag the orange circular handles on the graph

Manual straight line: 2 handles. Manual curve: 3 handles. Drag up or down to change the fit without moving your data points.

Optional extrapolationUse X-axis values

Enter the destination X value, not the extra distance. For measurements from X = 1 to X = 5, enter 0 to extend backwards or 7 to extend forwards. Leave a field blank for no extension. Extrapolated portions are shown with distinct dashed lines.

Only exclude a measurement for a sound experimental reason, not simply to improve R². Mark a point as an outlier to exclude it from all automatic regressions, including comparisons; manually adjusted lines stay where you put them. Automatic fits use least-squares regression. The solid fitted section spans the included measurements; use the optional extrapolation inputs to extend beyond this range. Especially for curved fits, extrapolated predictions may be unreliable. A fitted curve is not simply a line joining the points.
