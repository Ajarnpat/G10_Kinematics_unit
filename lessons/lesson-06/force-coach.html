WORKSHEET 4A–4D · UNDERSTANDING BEFORE CALCULATING

Understanding mass and weight

Mass

Mass measures the amount of matter in an object and its inertia (resistance to acceleration). Its SI unit is kilogram (kg); it stays the same when you travel to another planet.

Weight

Weight is the gravitational force on an object, measured in newtons (N). It acts toward the attracting body and changes when the gravitational field changes.

What is g?

Gravitational field strength is gravitational force per kilogram. Near Earth, g = 9.8 N/kg; near the Moon, g ≈ 1.62 N/kg. Ignoring air resistance, the same numerical values describe the acceleration in free fall, in m/s².

When finding weight, Fg = mg and mass must be entered in kilograms.

WORKSHEET Q5 · CALCULATE AND CHECK

Weight is gravitational force

Fg = mg

m = mass in kilograms; g = gravitational field strength in N/kg (numerically the same as m/s²); Fg = weight in newtons.

On Earth, g = 9.8 N/kg. On the Moon, g ≈ 1.62 N/kg. Mass does not change when you travel to the Moon; weight does.

WORKSHEET Q6–7 · VECTORS

Resultant (net) force

A force is a vector: it has a size and direction. Add forces in one straight line using signs (for example, right = positive).

Fnet = ΣF. If acceleration is zero, Fnet = 0. An object can be stationary or moving at constant velocity when forces balance. Otherwise its acceleration points in the net-force direction.

Newton’s second law: Fnet = ma.

WORKSHEET Q8 · QUICK REVEAL

How do we determine a force?

Reveal the three methods

1. Measure directly with a force meter (spring balance / newton scale).
2. Calculate weight using Fg = mg.
3. Use Newton’s second law, Fnet = ma; if acceleration is zero, the resultant force is zero and forces balance.
